Educational Crop Disease Guide

Explore three common agricultural diseases. Understand key visual markers, symptom patterns, and sustainable agronomic prevention protocols.

🍅 Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) Fungal Disease
Concentric Target Spots

Tomato Early Blight

Pathogen: Alternaria solani

Possible Symptoms:
  • Small brown to black spots developing primarily on older lower foliage.
  • Enlarging circular lesions exhibiting distinctive concentric rings ("target board" pattern).
  • Surrounding tissue often turns chlorotic (yellow halo) as spots coalesce.
General Prevention & Guidance:

Reduce prolonged leaf wetness by using drip irrigation instead of overhead sprinklers. Maintain suitable plant spacing and staking to maximize airflow. Remove infected lower leaves and practice at least 2–3 year crop rotations with non-solanaceous crops.

⚠️ Visual observation alone cannot confirm diagnosis. Consult agricultural extension services.
🥔 Potato (Solanum tuberosum) Oomycete Water Mold
Water-Soaked Lesions

Potato Late Blight

Pathogen: Phytophthora infestans

Possible Symptoms:
  • Irregular, dark brown to purplish water-soaked-looking lesions on leaf tips or margins.
  • Lesions expand rapidly under cool, moist weather, blighting whole leaflets within days.
  • Under high humidity, a delicate white cottony fungal-like bloom appears under leaves.
General Prevention & Guidance:

Monitor crops vigilantly during cool, damp weather. Plant certified disease-free seed tubers. Promptly destroy cull piles and volunteer potatoes that harbor inoculum. Seek rapid local agricultural advisory upon first regional blight forecast warnings.

⚠️ High-consequence pathogen. Immediate field inspection is advised if lesions are spotted.
🌾 Wheat (Triticum aestivum) Basidiomycete Fungus
Orange Powdery Pustules

Wheat Leaf Rust

Pathogen: Puccinia triticina

Possible Symptoms:
  • Small, round-to-oval orange-brown powdery pustules (uredinia) scattered on upper leaf blades.
  • Pustules rupture the leaf epidermis, releasing powdery spores that rub off easily.
  • In severe attacks, premature leaf senescence and impaired grain filling occur.
General Prevention & Guidance:

Plant locally recommended rust-resistant wheat varieties (with slow-rusting or adult-plant resistance genes). Eliminate volunteer green-bridge wheat plants before sowing. Follow regional crop surveillance alerts and university extension recommendations.

⚠️ Wind-borne spores can travel long distances. Always follow local agricultural advisories.
🌱 Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum) Bacterial Pathogen
Angular Vein-Bound Spots

Cotton Bacterial Blight

Pathogen: Xanthomonas citri pv. malvacearum

Possible Symptoms:
  • Small, water-soaked, angular lesions sharply delimited and bounded by minor leaf veins.
  • Lesions turn dark reddish-brown to purple-black with greasy margins on leaf undersides.
  • Can progress systemically along petioles and main stems to cause dark "blackarm" cankers.
General Prevention & Guidance:

Sow certified acid-delinted seeds to destroy seed-borne bacteria. Choose resistant cotton hybrids with proven genetic resistance. Plow under crop residue immediately following harvest and avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation that facilitates bacterial splashing.

⚠️ Seed-transmitted bacterial pathogen. Field hygiene and certified seed selection are primary defenses.
🌽 Corn / Maize (Zea mays) Fungal Foliar Blight
Cigar-Shaped Lesions

Northern Corn Leaf Blight

Pathogen: Exserohilum turcicum

Possible Symptoms:
  • Distinctive long, elliptical, "cigar-shaped" lesions (1 to 6 inches long) parallel to leaf margins.
  • Initially grayish-green and water-soaked, expanding and drying to a tan, straw-colored necrotic center.
  • In humid mornings, dense olive-gray dusty fungal spores form in the lesion centers.
General Prevention & Guidance:

Plant resistant corn hybrids equipped with Ht resistance genes and quantitative background tolerance. Rotate with non-host crops (such as soybeans, cotton, or legumes) for 1–2 seasons. Manage corn residue to accelerate fungal decomposition and scout fields regularly.

⚠️ Can cause severe yield loss if lesions develop on the ear leaf before or during pollination.
💡

Best Practices for Crop Health Management

Early intervention is crucial. Always examine the entire plant canopy, soil moisture balance, and weather history. For conclusive diagnosis, submit physical tissue specimens to your accredited state or university plant pathology diagnostic laboratory.

Academic Context

About CropCare AI

An engineering college project and personal portfolio deliverable demonstrating modern front-end web engineering, browser image manipulation, and 3D visual computing.

🎯

Project Purpose & Audience

CropCare AI was engineered to bridge accessible web technologies and agricultural awareness. It provides an intuitive platform where farmers, agricultural students, gardening hobbyists, and developers can explore crop pathology safely in their browser.

  • Target Users: Smallholder farmers, agronomy students, kitchen gardeners, and software engineering reviewers.
  • Educational Value: Teaches accurate symptom identification and non-chemical cultural control strategies.
💻

Technology Stack & Architecture

The application is implemented as an ultra-fast, zero-dependency static web application, engineered for accessibility and mobile responsiveness:

HTML5: Semantic Page Structure CSS3: Custom Variables, Grid, 3D Transforms Vanilla JS: File Validation & DOM State Three.js: 3D WebGL Bio-Model & Shaders

🔒 100% Client-Side Privacy: Selected images are processed entirely within browser memory and are never uploaded to any remote server.

🛡️

Prototype Limitations (Version 1.0)

Honesty and academic integrity are core principles of this project. CropCare AI Version 1.0 does not claim artificial intelligence inference capabilities:

  • No external AI model or cloud inference endpoint is executed in Version 1.0.
  • No fabricated disease predictions or misleading percentage scores are generated.
  • The analyzer workflow prepares users for future machine learning integration while delivering educational guides.
🚀

Future Version 2.0 Roadmap

Subsequent development phases will incorporate real deep learning inference and field assistance tools:

  • CNN Model Integration: Lightweight MobileNetV3 / ResNet architecture trained on validated agricultural datasets.
  • Python / FastAPI Backend: Secure RESTful API for inference and uncertainty scoring.
  • Regional Languages: Native support for Hindi and Marathi for rural accessibility.
  • Offline PWA: Progressive Web App caching for remote field operation.
